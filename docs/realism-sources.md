# Realism sources

Every number the simulation uses, where it came from, and the assumption made wherever published data
was missing. Code cites these sections as `[S1]`…`[S5]`.

The research record at the end was gathered by a multi-agent research pass and then re-checked entry by
entry by an independent fact-checking pass against the primary sources (October 2026). Entries marked
**assumption = yes** are reasoned estimates, not published values.

Units are SI unless a table says otherwise. "Reference athlete" means a 90 kg college football player.

How it is applied (`src/sim/anatomy/body.ts`):

- Masses scale with body mass; lengths scale with stature. The vertical chain (ankle → knee → hip → C7 →
  vertex) is scaled so the vertex lands exactly at stature, because de Leva's endpoint definitions overlap
  by about 1.5 %.
- Segment inertia = mass × (radius of gyration × length)²; for the foot the longitudinal axis is
  front–back.
- Trunk centres of mass are placed on the mid-plane between the hip centres (assumption: de Leva gives
  positions along the landmark line, not the antero-posterior offset).
- Endpoint choice: joint centre to joint centre wherever de Leva provides it (shank KJC–AJC; hand to the
  fingertip so the hand mesh and mass match).
- A whole-body check (unit test) confirms mass fractions sum to 100 % and that Minimum and Standard detail
  have identical total mass, COM and whole-body inertia (within 0.5 %).

Passive joint damping (N·m·s/rad): lumbar and thoracic 5 (trunk quick-release studies, ~11 total), hip 2,
knee 1 (Riener knee model), ankle 0.5, shoulder 1, elbow 0.3, wrist 0.1, toes 0.1, neck 0.5,
sternoclavicular 0.5. These match the recommended set in the research record; values for the shoulder
girdle and toes are assumptions. In the engine benchmark this damping was also what kept MuJoCo stable
under extreme random torques.

When segments are fused at Minimum detail, the surviving joint inherits the fused joint's range (spine
and neck chains add; the sternoclavicular range is added to shoulder abduction and flexion).

## S4 — Muscle strength and speed

Peak isometric torques for the reference athlete (90 kg). Each joint direction is scaled from the best
available source by body mass and by an athlete factor of about 1.4 over untrained young men. That factor
is anchored on NFL Combine quadriceps 316 N·m vs. 204 N·m isometric for young men. Torques scale with
(body mass / 90 kg)^(2/3) (allometric scaling) and with the strength slider.

Torque–velocity (Hill): available torque = T0 × f(ω/ωmax), curvature a/T0 = 0.3, eccentric plateau
1.3 × T0. Basis: Forrester & Pain 2009 elite athletes (curvature 0.16–0.5, eccentric 1.12–1.4, ωmax
18–36 rad/s); Thelen 2003 defaults (0.25, 1.4).

Max joint angular velocity ωmax (rad/s): hip flexion axis 25, other hip axes 20, knee 25, ankle 22 (inv/ev 18), toes
20, shoulder 30, elbow 30, forearm and wrist 25, girdle 12, spine and neck 10. Hip and knee come from Forrester &
Pain 2009 (18–36). Shoulder and elbow are conservative relative to throwing (elbow 43 rad/s in pitching,
Fleisig). The rest are assumptions.

Grip strength: 570 N per hand for the reference athlete (Leyk 2007 young men 541 N; football-player
estimate 550–620 N).

Speed targets for calibration: NFL Next Gen Stats top ball-carrier speeds 9.6–10.4 m/s (top-10 range
9.63–9.94 m/s); position estimates WR/DB 9.8, RB 9.6, LB 9.3, TE 9.2, QB 9.1, DL 8.9, OL 8.3 m/s. Ground
reaction at top speed: Weyand 2010 Fpeak 3.62 BW, Favg 2.08 BW, contact 0.108 s. Clark & Weyand 2014: first-half
stance 2.65 BW in sprinters.

Walk–run transition: people change gait near a Froude number v²/(g·h) of 0.5, h = hip height (Alexander 1989;
Kram, Domingo & Ferris 1997, also at reduced gravity); preferred transition speed about 2.0 m/s in adults
(Hreljac 1993: 2.06 m/s). The controller caps the walking reference speed at Froude 0.5 (2.2 m/s for the
reference athlete).

Jump (`Commands.jump`; added October 2026, not yet through the fact-checking pass):

| Quantity | Target | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Standing vertical jump (COM rise) | 0.6–0.75 m | NFL Combine vertical jump means ~0.7–0.9 m by position (McGee & Burkett 2003, J Strength Cond Res 17:6; Robbins 2010, J Strength Cond Res 24:2888), measured reach to reach; college players lower |
| Counter-movement dip, push time | ~0.3–0.4 m, ~0.25 s | force-platform counter-movement jumps (Linthorne 2001, Am J Phys 69:1198) |
| Propulsive ground force | ~2–3 BW | same; the stance push is 2 BW and the world's launch adds up to 1.7 g net while a foot is on the turf |
| One-foot running take-off | contact ~0.11–0.13 s, vertical speed ~3–3.5 m/s, ~4–5 BW | long jump take-off (Lees, Graham-Smith & Fowler 1994, J Appl Biomech 10:61); the launch cap off a run is 4 g |
| Landing peak ground force | ~3–6 BW from ~0.6 m | drop landings, soft to stiff (Zhang, Bates & Dufek 2000, Med Sci Sports Exerc 32:812) |

Measured (tests/jump.test.ts): standing 0.62 m (0.56 m on the minimum body), landing on the toes 3.4–4.6 BW. The legs
alone (no launch) reach ~0.30 m: joint-level Hill curves without tendon recoil fall short of a real jump's take-off
power, which is why the launch stands in, as it does for the dive. Known shortfall (assumption): the simulated sprint
stride touches down on the toes for only ~0.03 s, so a one-foot take-off at 10 m/s lifts the body ~0.1–0.17 m (a real
one ~0.4–0.5 m) and a jog's ~0.15–0.3 m; the hurdle's tuck still lifts the feet ~0.37–0.5 m, over a diver.

Hip thrust from lowered pads (`THRUST` in anim/procedural.ts; owner request 2026-10-18, not yet through the
fact-checking pass):

| Quantity | Target | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Trunk lean firing out of a low stance | ~40–50° forward over the first steps, rising toward ~20–25° by the third or fourth | sprint-start kinematics (block clearance and first steps; e.g. Mero, Komi & Gregor 1992, Sports Med 13:376; Slawinski et al. 2010, J Strength Cond Res 24:896); the thrust caps the pads' angle at 0.8 rad (~46°) and comes up 0.3 rad over it |
| Contact with the shoulder, head up and to the side | the near shoulder leads, the head goes past the ball carrier, never the crown first | tackling technique taught to cut head contact (USA Football Heads Up Football; World Rugby Tackle Ready; the helmet-first "spearing" rules, NCAA/NFHS since 1976) |
| Thoracic rotation, side bend; scapular protraction; neck extension | ±30°, ±20° (lumbar ±18°); ~15°; ~60° | the S3 joint ranges the body already has; the strike's turn (0.5 rad), side bend (0.6 rad) and neck targets stay within them |

Measured (tests/thrust.test.ts): from lowered pads (0.6 / 1) the trunk's mean lean through the drive is 15° / 38° (was
4° / 16°, standing up) and the COM's rise over its forward travel 0.03 / 0.07 (was 0.11 / 0.28); over 60 hits on a
standing or jogging-in opponent the pads take 0.72 of the contact impulse (was 0.50) and the head 0.00 (was 0.07).
Assumption: which shoulder leads is the brain's choice of the near one (`Commands.shoulder`); the forward foot's when
the opponent is dead ahead.

ordinary play (`tests/injury.test.ts`, design §6.6 rates), not cadaver failure values.
